Topic notes

1. Principles of Physiotherapy Practice

★★★★☆R 3 · B 1 · C 4
  • Definition of physiotherapy — learn three sources: the World Physiotherapy (WCPT) policy statement on the Description of Physical Therapy; the Government of India Model Curriculum Handbook (Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, Allied Health Section 2015-16); and the National Commission for Allied and Healthcare Professions (NCAHP) Act 2021, under which the physiotherapist can practise independently or as part of a multidisciplinary team, with a minimum qualification of a baccalaureate degree.
  • Scope of practice: settings — hospitals, rehabilitation centres, community-based rehabilitation, primary health centres, out-patient and private clinics, sports centres, workplaces, schools, hospices, education and research institutions.
  • General and professional competencies: patient care, medical/clinical knowledge, practice-based learning, interpersonal and communication skills, professionalism, systems-based practice (this six-domain framework follows the ACGME graduate-medical-education model).
  • Principles of Evidence-Based Practice (Sackett): the integration of best research evidence with clinical expertise and patient values. The 5-step process — mnemonic "the 5 A's": Ask (build a PICO question), Acquire (search the literature), Appraise (critical appraisal), Apply (integrate with clinical judgement and patient preference), Assess (evaluate the outcome).
  • History taking, assessment tests, patient communication, and documentation of findings; treatment planning and organisation.
  • Documentation using the ICF: impairments of body function/structure, activity limitations, participation restrictions, with environmental and personal factors as modifiers.
  • Standardized scales and tests in assessment; psychometric properties — reliability, validity, responsiveness (MCID, MDC) — and their interpretation in practice.
Examiner’s view

This is the identity topic of Paper I. Long answers usually ask for the definition and scope of physiotherapy (quote the NCAHP Act 2021 for the Indian legal context), the principles of evidence-based practice with its steps, or the use of the ICF in documenting an assessment. Note that the ICF and psychometric concepts also carry direct marks in MSK Paper III — study once, score twice.

2. Core Professional Values in Physiotherapy including Professional and Research Ethics

★★★☆☆R 2 · B 1 · C 3
  • World Physiotherapy Standards of Physical Therapy Practice guideline — the international benchmark for professional conduct and service standards.
  • Core professional values across countries and regions — a widely used list is the American Physical Therapy Association's seven: accountability, altruism, compassion/caring, excellence, integrity, professional duty, social responsibility (learn any six with one-line definitions).
  • The four principles of (bio)medical ethics — Beauchamp and Childress — mnemonic "A-B-N-J": Autonomy (informed consent, respect for patient decisions), Beneficence (act in the patient's best interest), Non-maleficence ("first, do no harm"), Justice (fair distribution of care).
  • ICMR National Ethical Guidelines (2017) for biomedical and health research involving human participants; the ICMR Policy on Research Integrity and Publication Ethics (RIPE) — plagiarism, authorship, conflict of interest.
  • Ethical issues in physiotherapy practice: informed consent, confidentiality of records, professional boundaries, scope-of-practice violations, advertising, and dual relationships.
  • Research ethics operational points: ethics committee (Institutional Review Board) approval, Declaration of Helsinki, informed consent process, vulnerable populations.
Examiner’s view

Expect either a short note on the ICMR guidelines or an essay on the four ethical principles illustrated with physiotherapy scenarios. The four principles with a clinical example each (a patient refusing a treatment plan for autonomy; over-treating beyond evidence for non-maleficence) is the standard full-marks structure.

3. Research Methodology and Biostatistics

★★★★★R 2 · B 3 · C 5
  • Conceiving the research question — mnemonic "FINER": Feasible, Interesting, Novel, Ethical, Relevant; frame it as PICO(T): Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome (Time).
  • Literature search: databases — PubMed/MEDLINE, CINAHL, Cochrane Library, and PEDro (the physiotherapy-specific evidence database); search strategy with MeSH terms, Boolean operators (AND/OR/NOT), and inclusion/exclusion criteria.
  • Observational designs: cross-sectional (prevalence at a point in time), case-control (starts with outcome, looks back — odds ratio), cohort (starts with exposure, follows forward — relative risk); enhancing causal inference (bias, confounding, stratification).
  • Experimental designs: the randomised blinded (controlled) trial — the gold standard for therapy questions; alternative designs (crossover, factorial, pragmatic, N-of-1).
  • Studies of diagnostic tests: sensitivity, specificity, predictive values, likelihood ratios (concepts shared with MSK Paper III).
  • Qualitative research: interviews, focus groups, thematic analysis, saturation; mixed methods.
  • Systematic reviews and meta-analysis: protocol (register with PROSPERO), PRISMA flow diagram, heterogeneity (I-squared), forest plot, fixed vs random effects.
  • Sampling: probability — simple random, stratified, cluster, systematic; non-probability — convenience, purposive, snowball, quota.
  • Sample size and power: type I error (alpha, false positive — usually 0.05), type II error (beta, false negative), power = 1 - beta (conventionally 80%), effect size.
  • Measurement: precision, accuracy, validity, reliability; operational definitions of variables (independent, dependent, confounding).
  • Biostatistics — descriptive: measures of central tendency (mean, median, mode) and spread (range, IQR, variance, SD); normal distribution — mnemonic "68-95-99.7" (percentages within 1, 2, 3 SD of the mean).
  • Inferential: hypothesis testing (null vs alternative; p-value), parametric tests — paired/unpaired t-test, ANOVA (with post-hoc corrections); non-parametric equivalents — Mann-Whitney U, Wilcoxon signed-rank, Kruskal-Wallis; categorical data — chi-square, Fisher's exact; correlation (Pearson r, Spearman rho) vs regression; data synthesis in qualitative designs.
  • Dissemination: writing a manuscript (IMRAD — Introduction, Methods, Results, And Discussion), clinical practice guidelines, research dissemination best practice.
Examiner’s view

With three blueprint questions — the heaviest topic in any paper of the course — this unit must be mastered. The syllabus maps almost line-for-line onto Hulley's Designing Clinical Research. Classic long answers: study designs with a worked example of each; sample size and power; the hierarchy of evidence; parametric versus non-parametric tests with when-to-use logic. Pair the diagnostic-accuracy statistics with MSK Paper III revision.

4. Exercise Physiology

★★★★★R 3 · B 2 · C 5
  • Fundamentals of human energy transfer: ATP as the energy currency; the three energy systems — phosphagen/ATP-PC (0-10 seconds), anaerobic glycolysis (to ~2 minutes), oxidative system (sustained) — with substrates and examples.
  • Nutrition: macronutrients (carbohydrate 4 kcal/g, protein 4 kcal/g, fat 9 kcal/g) and micronutrients; food energy and optimum nutrition for exercise; energy expenditure at rest (BMR/RMR — largest single component of daily expenditure) versus during activity.
  • Measuring energy-generating capacity: direct and indirect calorimetry, VO2max, ventilatory threshold, graded exercise testing, submaximal prediction tests.
  • Responses and adaptations to exercise and training by system: pulmonary (ventilation), cardiovascular (increased stroke volume, decreased resting heart rate, improved blood pressure response), neuromuscular (recruitment, rate coding), musculoskeletal (hypertrophy, fibre transitions, bone density), endocrine (acute versus chronic hormonal responses).
  • Body composition and its evaluation, obesity assessment, weight control through energy balance (intake vs expenditure).
  • Training the anaerobic systems (sprints, resistance — high intensity, short bouts) versus the aerobic system (continuous, interval training — FITT framework).
  • Training muscles to become stronger: overload, specificity, progression; neural versus hypertrophic adaptation.
  • Factors affecting physiological function: environment — heat, cold, altitude, pollution; special aids (ergogenic aids); influence of age and gender on exercise response and trainability.
Examiner’s view

Two blueprint questions plus direct recurrence in MSK Paper II (Physiological Basis of Human Movement) and Paper IV (endurance and functional training) make this a five-star topic. Learn the energy systems with their timelines, the kcal values, and one adaptation per system. The exam frequently asks for system-wise adaptations in a table format.

5. Electrophysiology

★★★★★R 3 · B 2 · C 5
  • Anatomy and physiology of the peripheral nerve and muscle: axon, myelin sheath, saltatory conduction, node of Ranvier; neuromuscular junction events (action potential → calcium influx → acetylcholine release → end-plate potential).
  • Nerve fibre classification: A-alpha (motor/proprioception), A-beta (touch), A-gamma (muscle spindle), A-delta (fast pain, temperature), B (preganglionic autonomic), C (slow pain, postganglionic) — conduction velocity falls from A to C.
  • Electrical properties of nerve and muscle: resting membrane potential (approximately -70 mV in nerve, -90 mV in muscle), depolarisation and the action potential, all-or-none law, refractory periods, accommodation; strength-duration curve — rheobase (minimum intensity with long duration) and chronaxie (duration at twice rheobase) — the classical clinical curve shapes (normal, denervated, partially denervated).
  • Instrumentation for neuromuscular electrical stimulation: stimulator components, monophasic versus biphasic pulsed currents, key parameters — frequency (pps/Hz), pulse duration/phase, amplitude, duty cycle, ramp-up.
  • Muscle plasticity in response to electrical stimulation: reversal of disuse atrophy, fibre-type shifts, maintenance of muscle mass when denervated (beware the parameter window — denervated muscle needs long pulse durations, milliseconds, not the microseconds used on innervated muscle).
  • Effects of electrical stimulation on body systems: muscle pump and circulation, pain modulation (gate control and opioid mechanisms), tissue healing currents.
Examiner’s view

Electrophysiology carries two blueprint questions and reappears as Electro Physics in MSK Paper II and electro modalities in Paper IV. The strength-duration curve with its normal and denervated shapes, nerve fibre classification, and NMES parameters are the exam staples. Draw the strength-duration curve with labelled rheobase and chronaxie — it is a recurring diagram question.

6. Pedagogy in Physiotherapy Education

★☆☆☆☆R 1 · B 0 · C 1

read once, no blueprint question

  • Competency-based education: defining competencies as measurable outcomes, assessment aligned to outcomes, the move from time-based to competency-based progression.
  • Adult learning theories (Knowles' andragogy): self-direction, experience, readiness, problem-centred orientation; learning styles (taught widely but not supported as an evidence-based teaching method); motivation (intrinsic vs extrinsic).
  • Intended learning outcomes: Tyler's principles (objectives derived from needs, expressed in behavioural terms); Bloom's taxonomy — mnemonic "K-S-A" for its Knowledge (cognitive), Skill (psychomotor), Attitude (affective) domains, each with its graded levels (e.g., remember → create in the revised cognitive domain).
  • Miller's pyramid of clinical competence — mnemonic "K-K-S-D": Knows → Knows how → Shows how → Does (the base is knowledge, the apex is real practice).
  • Dreyfus model of skill acquisition: novice → advanced beginner → competent → proficient → expert.
  • Instructional design and individual assessment: MCQ writing (single best answer, item stems, avoiding cueing), skill assessment (OSCE — objective structured clinical examination), oral presentations, rubrics and standardisation.
  • Instructional techniques: knowledge transfer (lectures, flipped classroom), skill development (demonstration, simulation, deliberate practice), attitudes (role modelling, reflection), teaching with technology (e-learning, virtual patients).
  • Academic planning and organisation: curriculum mapping, course design, feedback and evaluation.
Examiner’s view

The blueprint allots no separate question to pedagogy, so prepare it as short-note material only — Bloom's taxonomy, Miller's pyramid, and the Dreyfus stages are the three most quotable frameworks, and they also dress up any answer about postgraduate teaching competencies. Do not spend mastery-level time here.

7. Management, Entrepreneurship and Leadership in Physiotherapy Practice

★★☆☆☆R 1 · B 1 · C 2
  • Management — definition: the art and science of getting things done through people; principles and the classic functions — mnemonic "PODCC" (Fayol): Planning, Organising, Directing/Commanding, Coordinating, Controlling.
  • Evolution of management thought: scientific management (Taylor), administrative theory (Fayol), human relations movement (Mayo's Hawthorne studies), modern systems/contingency approaches.
  • Management process: planning (goals, budgets, protocols), organising (structure, staffing, delegation), directing (leadership, motivation, supervision), controlling (standards, audit, quality assurance); decision-making models (rational, shared, evidence-informed).
  • Responsibilities of the physiotherapy manager: staffing (recruitment, credentialing, orientation, appraisal), patient care oversight, fiscal responsibilities (budgeting, cost recovery, coding and billing), risk management (incident reporting, safety), legal and ethical responsibilities, communication responsibilities (team, patients, administration).
  • Entrepreneurship in physiotherapy: need, advantages and opportunities (growing demand, niche services, home care, corporate ergonomics), challenges and barriers in the Indian context (capital, insurance ecosystem, competition, regulatory awareness).
  • Leadership: need and relevance; theories — trait, behavioural, situational/contingency, transformational versus transactional; competencies and characteristics (vision, communication, emotional intelligence, team-building).
Examiner’s view

One blueprint question, usually a short note on the functions of management or the manager's responsibilities. Learn Fayol's functions with one physiotherapy-department example each, and be able to discuss entrepreneurship with an Indian private-practice example — evaluators reward context over textbook recitation.

Glossary of difficult terms (7)

Dotted-underlined words also show the definition on hover or tap.

  • atrophy — Wasting or shrinking of muscle or tissue.
  • hypertrophy — An increase in muscle or tissue size.
  • ICF — The International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health.
  • muscle spindle — A stretch sensor inside muscle that helps control posture and movement.
  • proprioception — The sense of body and joint position and movement.
  • sensitivity — A test's ability to correctly identify people who have the condition.
  • specificity — A test's ability to correctly identify people who do not have the condition.
